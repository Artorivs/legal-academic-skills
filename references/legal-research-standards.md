# Legal and academic research standards

Use this reference for legal research, doctrinal analysis, case synthesis, and scholarly writing. It is a quality-control guide, not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific professional judgment.

## 1. Scope and research question

- Identify the jurisdiction(s), court or tribunal level, relevant dates, governing legal regime, and research purpose.
- Convert the request into answerable sub-questions and state material assumptions.
- Distinguish descriptive research, doctrinal interpretation, comparative analysis, empirical claims, and normative argument.
- If jurisdiction, date, or procedural posture could change the result, ask for clarification or explicitly mark the assumption.

## 2. Source hierarchy and provenance

- Prefer primary sources for statements of law: legislation, regulations, judgments, official gazettes, treaties, and official decisions as applicable.
- Use secondary literature to map debates, locate authorities, and contextualise doctrine; do not silently substitute commentary for primary authority.
- Record the exact source, issuing body or author, date, stable URL or database identifier, and access date where relevant.
- Distinguish an official source, a reputable secondary reproduction, a search-result snippet, and an unverified reference.
- Never claim to have opened, read, checked, or verified a source unless that action actually occurred.

## 3. Authority, status, and time

For each important legal authority, record as applicable:

- jurisdiction and court/issuing body;
- date and procedural posture;
- precedential status and whether it is binding or persuasive for the question;
- subsequent treatment, amendment, repeal, overruling, reversal, or other status change;
- relevant paragraph, page, section, or pinpoint.

Do not infer that a case remains good law from its age, citation, or appearance in a secondary source. If current-status checking is unavailable, label it as unverified and say what check remains necessary.

## 4. Claim-to-source discipline

- Attach citations to the propositions they actually support.
- Verify quotations against the source text and preserve material qualifications and context.
- Use pinpoints where available. Do not invent page numbers, paragraph numbers, neutral citations, DOIs, quotations, or bibliographic details.
- Separate a source's express holding from dicta, factual findings, an author's interpretation, and the agent's own synthesis.
- If one source supports only part of a compound sentence, split the sentence or qualify the unsupported part.
- Mark citations needing verification rather than filling gaps by inference.

## 5. Counterarguments and completeness

- Search for contrary authority, exceptions, limiting principles, jurisdictional splits, and plausible alternative interpretations.
- Identify the strongest counterargument before recommending a conclusion.
- Explain why contrary evidence is distinguishable, less authoritative, outdated, or genuinely unresolved; do not omit it merely because it complicates the thesis.
- Distinguish a search not conducted from a search conducted without finding a result.

## 6. Academic integrity and argument quality

- Keep a visible chain from research question to method, evidence, analysis, and conclusion.
- Distinguish original analysis from paraphrase, quotation, and synthesis of other scholars.
- Do not fabricate sources, datasets, interviews, methods, results, or peer-review claims.
- Preserve the author's intended position during editing unless asked to reconsider it; flag substantive changes.
- Avoid overclaiming causation from correlation or generalising beyond the sample, sources, or jurisdiction examined.
- Follow the target journal, institution, citation style, ethics rules, and disclosure requirements where supplied.

## 7. Uncertainty labels

Use the labels defined in the `source-verification` skill so that every workflow and register speaks the same vocabulary:

- **Access mode:** live source, user-supplied source, source missing.
- **Source status:** Inspected, Partly inspected, Lead only, Unavailable, Inauthentic/incorrect.
- **Verification level reached:** Existence, Content, Paragraph.
- **Claim support:** Direct support, Partial support, Inference required, Interpolated, Quotation inaccurate, Contradicted, Not addressed, Not yet assessed.
- **Salience:** load-bearing, supporting, illustrative.

Mark the agent's own reasoning as inference or analysis, and record a research limitation whenever a source, database, date range, or jurisdiction could not be checked.

## 8. Final quality gate

Before delivering, check: scope; date and jurisdiction; primary-source support; authority status; pinpoints; quotation accuracy; contrary authorities; unsupported claims; citation completeness; limitations; and whether a qualified human reviewer is needed. Never present a generated draft as a substitute for professional legal advice or final scholarly verification.
