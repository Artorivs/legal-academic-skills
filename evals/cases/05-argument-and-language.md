# Case 05 — Seeded argument and language faults

**Target capability:** `argument-and-language-check`, standard depth.

## Input

A paragraph of about 250 words of legal analysis, written or adapted by the tester, with these faults planted and recorded privately before the run:

1. **Unstated, contested warrant:** the step from reason to claim relies on a principle a reader in the field would dispute (for example, that a duty recognised for courts applies to every administrative body).
2. **Overstrong qualifier:** the conclusion says "must" or "always" where the reasons support only "may" or "in some cases".
3. **Unrepresentative instance:** one decision cited for a general rule.
4. **Description in place of argument:** two sentences summarise an authority without saying what it shows for the claim.
5. **Mechanical cohesion:** three consecutive sentences open with "Moreover", "Furthermore", "In addition".
6. **Meaning-changing language error:** a modal or negation error in a statement of law (for example, "all decisions are not reviewable" for "not all decisions are reviewable").
7. **Article and agreement errors** of the kind common in second-language writing, at least three.

Leave one citation with a pinpoint in the paragraph, and leave several sentences correct, so that changes to fixed facts and false positives can be measured.

## Expected behaviour

- Argument shown in standard form, with the planted warrant supplied as an implicit premise and identified as contested.
- Faults 1–4 each identified and repaired, using the argument model's terms.
- Language scorecard with a band per criterion and quoted evidence; fault 5 under coherence and cohesion; fault 6 ranked above fault 7.
- Fault 7 reported as a pattern with a rule, not only corrected.
- Corrected passage keeps the author's position (narrowed where needed), and the citation and pinpoint unchanged.
- No band reported as an IELTS score.

## Critical failures

Changing the citation or pinpoint; replacing the author's thesis; inventing support for the unsupported step; reporting the result as an IELTS score.

Record how many planted faults were found, and how many correct sentences were changed without need.
