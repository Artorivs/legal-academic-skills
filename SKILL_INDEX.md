# Skill Index

- [`academic-project-router`](skills/academic-project-router/SKILL.md) — Choose and coordinate the appropriate academic legal research or writing workflow. Use when a task spans several kinds of work or the next step is unclear.
- [`research-planning`](skills/research-planning/SKILL.md) — Design a rigorous research plan for academic legal work, including research questions, scope, method, source strategy, and manageable research stages; or assess whether an existing thesis or article idea is novel, worth pursuing, and sound enough to build on.
- [`source-verification`](skills/source-verification/SKILL.md) — Verify legal, academic, empirical, and archival sources and test whether each supports the exact proposition it is cited for, either for one source before relying on it or across a whole manuscript's footnotes, quotations, pinpoints, and bibliography.
- [`legal-authority-analysis`](skills/legal-authority-analysis/SKILL.md) — Analyse cases, legislation, treaties, international legal materials, and administrative decisions with attention to authority, context, reasoning, limits, and competing readings.
- [`literature-synthesis`](skills/literature-synthesis/SKILL.md) — Synthesize legal and interdisciplinary scholarship into an analytical account of debates, methods, evidence, and contribution rather than a sequence of source summaries.
- [`argument-mapping`](skills/argument-mapping/SKILL.md) — Map an academic legal thesis into linked claims, evidence, warrants, assumptions, counterarguments, and vulnerabilities before drafting or revising.
- [`academic-legal-drafting`](skills/academic-legal-drafting/SKILL.md) — Turn notes, sources, outlines, or rough prose into connected academic legal writing with a clear claim, developed reasoning, and careful source use.
- [`manuscript-review`](skills/manuscript-review/SKILL.md) — Review a legal academic manuscript as an editor, peer reviewer, examiner, or pre-submission auditor, and report consequential weaknesses ranked by severity with specific remedies. Use for a desk read, a full referee report, or a final audit before submission.
- [`adversarial-writing-cycle`](skills/adversarial-writing-cycle/SKILL.md) — Run a repeatable draft–critique–response–revision–retest cycle to expose weaknesses in an academic legal argument and record unresolved objections. Also use to work through external referee reports, editor letters, or co-author comments and draft a response to reviewers.
- [`multilingual-legal-writing`](skills/multilingual-legal-writing/SKILL.md) — Draft, revise, or translate legal scholarship in or between any languages, and work with foreign-language legal sources, while preserving doctrinal meaning, jurisdiction-specific terminology, and scholarly register.
- [`manuscript-consolidation`](skills/manuscript-consolidation/SKILL.md) — Consolidate a manuscript by removing repetition, improving structure and transitions, and preserving necessary evidence, qualifications, and argumentative steps; or bring it under a word limit by relegating non-essential material to footnotes.
- [`project-continuity`](skills/project-continuity/SKILL.md) — Continue a long academic research project across sessions or models using stable records, explicit decisions, corrections, evidence gaps, and concise handovers.
- [`anticipatory-analysis`](skills/anticipatory-analysis/SKILL.md) — Create and apply a pre-specified protocol for analysing future events or incoming evidence without retrofitting the method to the outcome.
