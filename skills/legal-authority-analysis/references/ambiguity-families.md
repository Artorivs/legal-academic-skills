# Ambiguity families for legislative and treaty text

Use these when the meaning of a provision carries weight in the argument. Families overlap; aim for coverage, not a clean classification.

| Family | Question to ask | Typical trigger words |
|---|---|---|
| Internal contradiction | If both provisions are applied literally, is the result impossible or opposite? Watch general and specific provisions on the same subject. | notwithstanding, supersedes, except as, subject to |
| Vague operative term | Does an undefined word switch a right or duty on or off, so that two honest readers could draw the line differently? | reasonable, appropriate, substantial, significant, promptly, necessary |
| Definitional boundary | Can you describe a realistic case that is arguably inside and arguably outside a defined term? | including, such as, and the like, means and includes |
| Standardless discretion | Who decides, on what standard, and what stops a self-serving decision? Is there review? | as it considers, in its discretion, satisfied that, may determine, deems |
| Gap or silence | For each required step, what governs if it is skipped, partial, or impossible? For each duty, is the consequence of breach stated? | to be prescribed, to be determined, from time to time |
| Cross-provision tension | Does a power granted in one place erode a protection promised in another, including a lower instrument eroding a higher one? | pursuant to, in accordance with, may amend or vary |

Add a seventh check across the whole text: is each defined term used consistently, and do undefined near-synonyms appear that could be argued to mean something different?

For each ambiguity that matters, state the competing readings, the interpretive arguments for each (text, context, purpose, structure, drafting history, and, for treaties, the Vienna Convention means of interpretation), how courts or bodies have resolved it if they have (checked, not recalled), and what turns on the choice for the paper's argument.
